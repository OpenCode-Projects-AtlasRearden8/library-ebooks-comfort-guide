# Glossary

Plain definitions for every term this guide uses, alphabetical. Where a term has a specific control in one of the apps, the definition says where to find it.

BingePass
:   A hoopla borrow type that opens an entire collection — 100+ magazines, The Great Courses, documentary or comics collections — with a single instant borrow. Unlimited access within that collection for 7 days; costs one of your monthly instant borrows.

Borrow
:   To check a digital title out to your account. Loans last a fixed period (usually 7, 14, or 21 days, set by the library) and then return themselves. There are no late fees.

Borrowing limit
:   The maximum number of titles you can have checked out at once. Libby queues are commonly capped around 10 loans; hoopla instant borrows are a monthly allowance rather than a shelf. Both are set by your library.

Card number
:   The digits that identify your library account (usually around 14; length varies). Paired with a PIN, it signs into Libby, hoopla, and your library's website.

Checked out / Checked out to someone else
:   The one-copy-one-reader state: every license is in use, so new readers see **Place Hold** instead of **Borrow**.

Consortium
:   A group of libraries sharing one digital collection and catalog. Your small-town card often sign-ins through a larger regional network's app name — same card, shared shelf.

Deliver later
:   A Libby hold option: keep your place in queue but skip the "your hold is ready!" notification until a date you choose. Distinct from suspending (below). Useful when the hold arrives during a trip.

DRM (digital rights management)
:   The copy protection wrapped around library files. It's why library ebooks can't simply be copied to other apps, and why Kindle loans only flow through Amazon's delivery page.

Early return
:   Giving a loan back before its due date. Frees the copy for the next reader — and on hoopla, frees a monthly borrow slot for you. Polite, no fees, zero ceremony.

e-card / digital access card
:   A library card issued entirely online (instant number+PIN) for digital materials; whether you must visit the library in person to make it a full card depends on each library's rules.

EPUB
:   The standard ebook file format. Library EPUBs are DRM-wrapped; personal EPUBs you own can be sent to a Kindle with Amazon's own "Send to Kindle" tool (a different feature from Libby's Kindle delivery — chapter 04).

Fire tablet
:   Amazon's tablet line. No Google Play — Libby installs from the Amazon Appstore. Kindle delivery doesn't apply; on Fire tablets you read in Libby directly.

Fixed layout
:   An ebook format where the page is an image-like layout (picture books, comics, many magazines). Zoom is the large-text mechanism for these (up to about 3.5× in Libby).

Flex borrow (hoopla)
:   The hoopla license type used for newer bestsellers: one-copy-one-reader with its own monthly allowance. Shows as Available or Wait List with revolving arrows.

Hold
:   Your reservation in a title's waiting list. When a copy frees up, you get a short window (commonly 2–3 days) to borrow it before the offer passes to the next reader.

Hold limit
:   The maximum number of holds you can have at once on one card (often around 5–15). Set per library.

Instant borrow (hoopla)
:   hoopla's no-wait license type: any number of readers can borrow the same title simultaneously. Marked with a lightning-bolt symbol. Counts against your monthly instant allowance.

Kindle
:   Amazon's reading platform, in three shapes this guide distinguishes: the **Kindle app** (phone/tablet), **Kindle e-readers** (Paperwhite, and friends), and **Fire tablets** (which read in Libby, not via Kindle delivery).

Content & Devices
:   Amazon's online area for managing your ebooks and devices (Account & Lists → Content & Devices). The re-delivery fix for misbehaving Kindle loans lives there.

Loan period
:   How long a borrow lasts: 7, 14, or 21 days typically, chosen by your library per format and title. The loan ends — not "expires" with a penalty — automatically.

Lucky Day / skip-the-line copies
:   Short-term instant copies of bestsellers some libraries buy specially for popular lines. Search your library's catalog for the phrase or ask a librarian.

Magazine BingePass
:   hoopla's single-borrow pass opening 100+ popular magazines (current issues) for 7 days. Available in the U.S., Canada, Australia, and New Zealand.

Multi-card
:   Holding more than one library card (e.g., home-town plus a big-city non-resident card) in Libby or hoopla — legitimately, per each library's eligibility rules. Two queues for the same book, two chances to borrow, and each card keeps its own limits.

Offline download
:   Saving a stream/copy to your device so reading/listening works without data. Both apps offer it per-title; do it on Wi-Fi before trips.

One-copy-one-user
:   The standard license model — like a physical book that can only be "in the hands" of one reader at a time. The reason waitlists exist. Contrast with simultaneous-use.

OpenDyslexic
:   A typeface with weighted bottoms and clear letter separation, designed to help some readers with dyslexia. Available in Libby's Book Design, the Kindle font list, and (on many models) Kobo's settings. Try it for a chapter; it isn't a treatment — a comfort setting.

OverDrive
:   The company behind Libby and the digital catalogs used by most public libraries. Older handbooks say "OverDrive app" and "OverDrive books" — the app line was retired and everything moved to Libby.

Renew
:   Extending a loan before it ends, when the system allows it (no holds behind you, and possibly a renewal cap). The alternative to a renewal is re-borrowing after return.

Send to Kindle
:   Two different things, confused constantly: (1) Libby's **Read With Kindle** delivery for *library* loans (U.S. libraries); (2) Amazon's own **Send to Kindle** tool for sending *your personal documents* (EPUB/PDF/DOCX, up to ~200 MB) to your Kindle apps/devices. Library files are DRM-protected and can only travel via path (1).

Simultaneous-use license
:   A license allowing unlimited readers at once — no waitlists ever. Common for classics and hoopla Instant titles; some libraries buy these for book-club favourites.

Suspend a hold
:   Freezing your hold position for a set period (travel, exams) instead of losing your place. Your spot resumes when you unsuspend. hoopla Flex holds queue automatically; Libby offers explicit suspend/deliver-later controls.

Sync
:   The behind-the-scenes updated handshakes between your devices and the service's servers: loans appearing, last-read positions, hold status. When sync misbehaves, the fixes are chapter 08's — light on mystery, heavy on sign-out/sign-in.

Text scale
:   Libby's zoom-for-text control inside the reader. Turning on **Include accessibility sizes** reveals the truly large sizes.

Waitlist / Queue
:   The ordered list of people waiting for a one-copy-one-reader title. Movement is lumpy (a batch of expirations), not daily. Expected wait ≈ (people ahead ÷ copies) × loan length — an estimate, not a promise.

Whispersync
:   An Amazon feature syncing reading *and* audio positions for **paid Amazon** books (and adding professional narration options). Library loans don't use Whispersync; their positions still sync through the Kindle app and Libby separately.
