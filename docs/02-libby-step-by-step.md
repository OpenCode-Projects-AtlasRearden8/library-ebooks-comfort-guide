# Libby, Step by Step

Libby is the app most libraries in the U.S., Canada, and many other countries use for ebooks, audiobooks, and digital magazines. It's friendly once you know four or five taps — the trick is that the first run hides a few choices that set everything up. This chapter walks the whole road: install, sign in, borrow, read, renew, and keep two or three libraries in one account.

Every UI detail below was accurate at time of writing (October 2026) against Libby's official help pages. Apps drift; when a button has moved, it will still be whatever's named the same thing.

## One request: install it even if you think it's complicated

Libby's installed one way: your device's app store (Apple App Store on iPhone/iPad; Google Play on Android). On any computer, you can skip the app altogether and use the same interface in a browser at `libbyapp.com` — the tools are identical, just laid out for a bigger screen.

Fire tablets come without Google Play — open the **Amazon Appstore** instead and install Libby from there.

> **No card yet?** Chapter 01 gets you one — often in minutes.

## First run: the three taps that matter

Open Libby. What follows is a guided interview — your library, then your card, then your defaults.

1. It asks **"Do you have a library card?"** — tap **I Have a Library Card**. (If you tap the wrong option accidentally, there's an escape hatch at every screen: something labelled **Skip** or the ✕ at top.)
2. **Find your library.** Type your city, your library's name, or your ZIP code. Libby shows a list; pick the one that matches your card. If your library belongs to a regional network, you'll see the network's name and possibly a list of member libraries — pick the network, not the branch.
3. **Sign into your card.** Tap **Enter Library Account Details**, then type your card number and PIN. Libby remembers this from here on.

That's the whole setup. You'll land on the library's "digital shelf."

## Borrowing your first ebook or audiobook

The home screen is a browse screen, organized the way a library is organized: covers, curated collections ("New this week," "Popular," kids-only sections), and a search box at the top. Here's the fast start:

1. Tap the **search box** at the top and type part of a title, an author's name, or a genre. Or scroll around the curated sections — they're deliberately browsable, not an alphabet soup.
2. **Filter to available now** (this saves real time): open the filters (often a **Filters** button above the results) and check **Available Now**. Libby will hide anything with a waitlist and show only titles you can borrow immediately.
3. Tap a cover → tap **Borrow!**
4. Libby shows the loan length — usually **14 or 21 days** (your library sets it). Tap **Borrow!** again to confirm.
5. Now it's on your shelf. Tap **Read in Libby** for an ebook, or **Listen in Libby** for an audiobook, and you're off.

Some titles say **Place Hold** instead of **Borrow!** — that means the copies are checked out. Chapter 05 covers holds honestly (spoiler: lines move, and you can hold the same book at two systems at once without breaking anything).

## Reading and listening without drama

Two things to know about the Libby reader:

- **Tap the middle of a page.** This reveals the controls: practice that tap-then-tap rhythm once and everything else falls into place.
- **Reading settings** is where font size, page colour (light/sepia/dark), brightness, and the dyslexia-friendly font live. Full walkthrough in chapter 09.

Audiobooks work as you'd hope: press play, and the player shows a **speed control** (Libby offers playback speeds from roughly 0.6× up to 3× — handy for slowing down a dense narrator or speeding past a slow one), a **sleep timer** (for the people-who-fall-asleep contingent: yes, we exist), and **skip 15 seconds** for rewinding past a missed line. For the car, Libby works over Bluetooth, Apple CarPlay, and Android Auto.

Audiobooks carry your position between devices. Borrow on your phone, listen in the car on the same card: wherever you left off carries over within a few seconds of opening. (If it ever doesn't: chapter 08 has the sync fixes.)

## The shelf — your control room

Tap **Shelf** (bottom of the screen) and you're looking at everything attached to your account:

- **Loans** — what you currently have. Tap a loan to open: **Read Now/Listen Now**, **Return Early**, or (when your library allows) **Renew**.
- **Holds** — your positions in waitlists (chapter 05 explains cancelling, *suspension*, and "Deliver later").
- **Tags** — Libby's free-form filing. Tag something "to-read" with one tap.
- **Notifications** — author alerts, library announcements, and hold-ready notices.

Two shelf-level habits worth having:

1. **Return early things you finished.** It's polite to the system and the line: the next reader gets the copy immediately, and digital copies have no tardiness penalty — so delaying return buys nobody anything.
2. **Open Libby whenever you add a new device.** The account is server-side; the app on the new phone/tablet syncs down your loans, holds, tags, and reading positions automatically after you sign in.

## The loan flow, end to end

The pattern to internalize:

- **Borrow** → becomes a **loan** (it's *yours* until the due date) → either **read/listen** in Libby on any of your devices that use *the exact same* Libby account, or (for ebooks, U.S. libraries) **send to Kindle** (chapter 04).
- **At the due date → the loan goes back by itself (auto-return).** Nothing to remember. If you were mid-book, Libby remembers your last position for when you re-borrow.
- If you finish early → **Return Early** to release the copy for the next reader.
- If you need more time → try **Renew** (if no one's behind you in line); if renewal's unavailable, the fallback is re-borrowing after the loan expires (a short gap sometimes; exact timing depends on your library's rules).

Renewals, re-borrows, and why a loan might say "not renewable" — that's the whole of chapter 06. Nothing there is a mystery once you've seen it once.

## Set your Kindle preference once (if it exists)

In **Settings** → **Preferences** (or in a popup offered after your first borrow), you can choose where *new ebooks* open by default: **in Libby**, or **send to Kindle**. If you send to Kindle by preference and one day a book absolutely refuses to go (rare, publisher-restricted), open the book's menu and choose **Read in Libby** as a one-off. Chapter 04 deals with this in detail so it never surprises you.

## Adding a second library card (the "multi-card" trick)

The Libby app supports multiple library cards. Common legitimate setups:

- Your home-town **library** card plus a **state-wide** or regional card.
- Two-town households: one card per town (both can be in Libby at once).
- A card for yourself and a separate one for the kids, so each family member's holds don't mix.

To add: **Settings** (☰ menu) → **Library Cards** → **Add Another Library** (wording varies slightly by version). Whichever is marked the active/default card is what you're borrowing under; switch by tapping a different card. Each card keeps its own loans, holds, and borrowing quotas.

> **One honest caution:** don't add a card you're not entitled to. Handfuls of big-city libraries accept out-of-town members (with or without a fee) — chapter 06 explains the ones that charge and the ones that don't, and our companion directory keeps an up-to-date list. The "trick" is playing within the rules to double your queues, not ignoring the queue rules.

## Small things that make Libby feel personal

- **Deep search for your next read:** the **Search** box isn't a dumb matcher. Try the author's first and last name only, or a series name. And when you find a keeper, tag it — future-you will thank present-you.
- **Tags for "not now but someday":** if a book strikes you as tempting while you're buried in a loan, tag it. No next-three-books decision fatigue.
- **Default actions:** Libby can be set to always open new ebooks in the reader, or always start playing an audiobook, so noisy situations (driving, walking) don't require re-choosing each time.
- **"Expires soon" notification:** Libby warns you if a hold you've been offered is about to expire without borrowing it (there's a short borrow-window after a hold comes in — chapter 05 explains it, and it's easy to miss).
