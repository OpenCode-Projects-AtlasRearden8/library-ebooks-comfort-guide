# hoopla, Step by Step

If Libby is your library's virtual branch, hoopla is the all-you-can-browse counter that never has a line for most things: ebooks, audiobooks, comics, movies, TV, music, BingePasses. The trade-off is a **monthly borrow allowance** set by your library, and a catalog that leans "available right now" rather than "everything ever published." This chapter gets you set up and teaches the differences that trip people up.

## What makes hoopla different

Three real differences, stated plainly:

- **Most items are instant.** Anything marked with a lightning-bolt style symbol borrows with no wait, any time, even if every other hoopla member in town borrowed it the day before. The library pays a small fee per borrow; you pay nothing.
- **You get a fixed allowance.** Your library sets how many *instant* borrows you get per month (field reports range from a handful to dozens — the number shows right on your home screen once you're signed in). Unused borrows typically don't roll over. (Some libraries grant none at all — budgeting choice, not an insult.)
- **"Flex" titles sometimes have queues.** Newer bestsellers are licensed one-copy-at-a-time like Libby. Those get the revolving-arrows symbol, show **Available** or **Wait List**, and have their own borrow allowance.

Loan lengths depend on the content type and your library's settings; as a rule of thumb, ebooks/audiobooks/comics sit around 21 days, music around 7, and video is short (a day or a few 48-hour windows per episode). Expiry is automatic — nothing is ever "late."

## Setup in five minutes

1. **Install the app** — Apple App Store, Google Play, or the Amazon Appstore for Fire tablets. A browser version also exists at `hoopladigital.com` (good for reading ebooks on a laptop and for watching on a TV browser).
2. **Tap Get Started** and choose your library from the list (search by name; your library's short name is usually enough).
3. **Enter your card number and PIN** from the library's side, then set up your hoopla account with an **email and password**. (Yes — hoopla wants its own password on top of the card details. Same advice as always: password manager, not a note on the refrigerator.)
4. Look at the **top of the home screen** (or your profile page): it shows *instant* borrows remaining and — if your library turned it on — *Flex* borrows remaining. Those numbers are your whole allowance model; get familiar with them in the first minute to avoid later "why can't I borrow?" confusion.

> **No subscription on your card?** If your library isn't on hoopla, that's a licensing decision, not a card problem. The companion directory *Free Reading for Less* keeps a current list of large-city libraries open to non-residents — a card from one of those often unlocks hoopla too.

## Browsing and borrowing

The catalog is searchable and category-organized (Ebooks, Audiobooks, Comics, Movies, TV, Music, BingePasses, Magazines). The flow:

1. Find a title by search or browsing.
2. Tap the cover, then **Borrow**. Instant titles become playable immediately; Flex titles either borrow (if marked Available) or let you **place a hold**.
3. Tap **Play** to start. That's it — no separate "checkout cart."

One pleasant detail: **one hoopla account attaches to one card**, so each family member's own card means their own allowance — the practical answer for a family sharing a tablet is separate cards in separate hoopla accounts (where the library allows it).

## Kids mode (worth two minutes if you have kids)

hoopla includes a KIDS mode that filters the catalog to children-appropriate content (picture books, kids' audiobooks, comics, animation). Switch it on from your profile/settings area; it's per-account rather than per-device, so as before, a kid's card of their own is the cleaner separation if your library allows multiple cardholders. Kids instinctively tap things; KIDS mode makes those taps harmless.

## The formats, in one honest table

| Format | Loan ticks | Wait to expect | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ebooks | ~21 days | None (Instant), possible (Flex) | Downloadable for offline |
| Audiobooks | ~21 days | None (Instant), possible (Flex) | Sleep timer, speed control |
| Comics/graphic novels | ~21 days | None (Instant) | hoopla's strong suit — deep catalog |
| Music | ~7 days | None | Whole albums; not a Spotify replacement but solid |
| Movies/TV | Very short windows | None | episode-based caps; per-title availability varies |
| BingePasses | 7 days of unlimited access to one collection | None | magazines, Great Courses, puzzles, more (below) |
| Magazines | via Magazine BingePass | None | current issues, US/CA/AU/NZ |

Exact lengths are **your library's settings** and can change; treat the table as orientation, not gospel. The app always shows the exact loan length before you confirm a borrow.

## BingePass — the strangest word on the menu, defined

A **BingePass** is a single borrow that opens a whole collection for 7 days, unlimited within that collection. Common examples: the **Magazines BingePass** (100+ popular magazines), **The Great Courses**, comics collections, puzzles, and streaming-service-style collections that rotate. You're spending one instant borrow for seven days of access, so on the math, it's almost always the best deal in the app. If you see a BingePass and a regular borrow competing for your month, the BingePass usually wins.

## Flex, honestly

Flex is hoopla's answer to "but I want the brand-new bestseller." It behaves like a mini-Libby:

- A Flex title either borrows now (if copies are free) or offers a **waitlist hold**, with hoopla notifying you when a copy frees up.
- Flex borrows have their **own allowance** separate from instant borrows. Running out of instant borrows doesn't prevent a Flex borrow and vice versa.
- If you run out of *Flex* borrows, returning one early frees a slot.

Waiting lists on Flex exist; they're typically shorter than the multi-hundred-person Libby queues for a runaway bestseller, but they're real. If a hot title is waitlisted on both Libby and hoopla Flex at your library, chapter 05 walks through your honest options.

## When hoopla doesn't have what you want (a healthy expectation)

hoopla's catalog is enormous but it's *not* the full commercial ebook market. Cases you'll hit:

- A specific current bestseller is on Libby at your library but not on hoopla. Solution: borrow it there instead; the two services coexist.
- Publishers sometimes withhold big new books from *any* instant-borrow model. That's a licensing standoff, not a trick your library is playing on you.
- Everything you *do* find (older titles, midlist, series, comics) is almost always available immediately.

That's the pattern for the whole book: between **Libby** (deep recent catalog, long queues for bestsellers) and **hoopla** (deeper older catalog, instant borrows, and creative formats like comics and BingePasses), virtually anything mainstream published in the last few years is reachable free — the "when" is what varies.
