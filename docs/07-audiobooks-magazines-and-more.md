# Audiobooks, Magazines, and More

Ebooks get all the attention, but the same card unlocks a library drawer most people never open: audiobooks with honest speed controls, current magazines, hundreds of comics, entire music albums, and streaming-style "BingePasses." This chapter tours the non-book parts — with the controls that make them pleasant on real trips, real commutes, and real couches.

## Audiobooks: the 15-minute learn

Both apps handle audiobooks well. The controls worth knowing (naming varies slightly by version; the functions don't):

- **Speed.** Libby offers playback from about 0.6× up to 3× (a wide ladder in small steps — fine-tuned for narrators who sip vs. narrators who sprint). hoopla has a speed control too. Slower speeds help on dense nonfiction; slightly-faster is the audiobook-lover's classic.
- **Skip and rewind.** Discrete jumps (Libby uses 15-second skips; hoopla similar) — the fast-forward buttons for "skip the narrator's recap."
- **Sleep timer.** Stops playback after 5/10/15/etc. minutes. If you listen in bed, make this your default gesture; your earbuds' "next chapter at 3 a.m." voice thanks you.
- **Bookmarks.** Pin the chapter you fell asleep on. (Libby remembers the exact second you stopped; hoopla too. The timer + bookmark combo is the sleeping-reader's whole toolkit.)
- **Bluetooth/CarPlay/Android Auto.** In the car, both apps surface *play/pause and skip* on the dash or steering wheel controls when playing over Bluetooth; chapter 10's car checklist has the two-minute setup.

Progress syncs within each app across your devices (same account), **within the app you borrowed them on** — a book borrowed on Libby syncs in Libby; hoopla books sync in hoopla. Loans never cross between the two services.

### Download vs. stream

On phones/tablets, both apps can **download a title for offline use** (needed for planes, metros, and basements). Streaming only spends data and causes the classic "audiobook buffering at 60 mph" problem. Before a trip: open the app on Wi-Fi, tap the loan, choose the **download/offline** option, and let it finish while you make coffee.

### The commuter pattern that works

1. Hold the big audiobooks you want (queues on audio are usually shorter than ebook queues — chapter 05).
2. *Meanwhile*, borrow any available audio you don't mind hearing at 1.5×; no loan-slot guilt; return early freely.
3. Keep **one** audiobook downloaded offline as your backup. Lines go down, tunnels happen, and a backup audiobook has saved more commutes than any gadget this decade.

## Magazines

Yes, current magazines — the same ones from the drugstore rack — free:

- **Through Libby:** many library systems carry digital magazines. Periodically they're outside the ordinary loan counting system (they're typically *not* counted against ebook+audiobook limits, though the exact accounting varies by library). Current issues show up near print release; back issues linger for the recent run. You read them in Libby's magazine reader — a zoomable, page-based view that works far better on a tablet or phone than you'd expect.
- **Through hoopla's Magazines BingePass:** one instant borrow opens 100+ magazines (woman's interest, news, tech, hobby — the current list is on hoopla's side) with unlimited access within the pass for 7 days. If you're a once-a-month someone-else-reads-them-too household, one borrow covers everyone.

A magazine is a *loan*, so treat it like the calendar says: borrow the issues you'll actually read, and skip the guilt stack of 14 borrowed issues you'll never open.

## Comics and graphic novels

hoopla is secretly one of the best free comic resources anywhere: a deep catalog of Marvel? No — mainstream American publishers come and go by licensing; hoopla's strength is the **breadth of licensed graphic novels, manga, and indie comics** across dozens of publishers, borrowable instantly with no queue, readable in a panel-by-panel reader designed exactly for small screens. Chapter 03's table covers loan details. Libby also carries graphic novels for systems that license them (fixed-layout titles with a zoomable reader).

Start with anything; the format lends itself to "borrow three, sample two, love one."

## Music, movies, and TV

Available on hoopla in many libraries (Libby doesn't do these):

- **Music:** full albums, roughly-a-week loans, instant. Not a Spotify replacement; excellent for "I want to hear that 1970s folk record everyone mentions" without a subscription.
- **Movies/TV:** rotating catalog, very short loan windows (often 48–72 hours per episode/title), instant. Best for "one specific movie tonight," not for ambient streaming. Availability rotates frequently — check what's there rather than what you hope is there.
- **BingePasses:** the 7-day unlimited pass concept again (Great Courses, documentaries, puzzle services, and more). One borrow, one week, whole collection. Chapter 03 defined it; try any that match a hobby week.

## Sample the advanced moves

Two reader-quality-of-life notes that audio/magazine people specifically appreciate:

- **Libby audiobook player colour/dark mode** follows your app's dark-mode setting; the player works fine one-handed at night.
- **Magazine articles can be searched** inside Libby's magazine reader — useful for the "I read that somewhere last month" moments.

## A calm note on formats

You don't need to use all of this. The honest hierarchy for a beginner:

1. ebooks and/or audiobooks (chapter 02/06) — most reading happens here;
2. magazines — surprise favourite of most people who try them;
3. comics/hoopla extras — the fun drawer.

Pick the formats you'll genuinely use, and ignore the rest without ceremony. Every borrow you *don't* make is budget the library spends on someone else's great book — the range is the point, not a to-do list.
