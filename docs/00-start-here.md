# Start Here: Free Library Ebooks, Explained Like a Friend Would

Free books. Free audiobooks. Free magazines with today's-and-last-month's luxury gloss. All from your library, all on devices you already own, and all without a subscription, a trial, or a credit card.

The catch? Nobody hands you a map. So this is the map: an honest, plain-English guide to getting started and staying comfortable, from your very first library card through fixes for every "the book won't load!" tantrum your app might throw at you.

## Who this guide is for

Anyone who wants free reading with less friction: a parent setting up a phone for a kid, someone who just got their first e-reader, a person reading with larger text, someone switching between a phone, a Kindle, and a library card that expires in two years. You don't need to be technical. Every step assumes you're starting smarter than a mouse and slower than a rabbit.

## The quick truth (read this first)

- **Nothing here costs money.** Library digital borrowing is genuinely free. The library pays a licensing fee when you borrow; you pay nothing.
- **You need two things**: a library card (or a digital/library access number) and a device. That's it.
- **Waitlists are real.** Popular titles get a line. This guide explains how lines work, and what's actually under your control — because you *do* have cards to play and more copies waiting than you might think.
- **Apps change.** Libby and hoopla tweak menus from time to time. When we say "tap the middle of the screen, then Reading Settings," that phrasing was accurate at the time of writing (October 2026). If something has moved, the intent is unmistakable — find the same feature with the same name, in whatever new spot it landed.

> **What this guide won't do:** sell you anything, mirror app screenshots you've already seen, or pretend your library's catalog looks exactly like your neighbor's. Libraries have different platforms, different borrowing strategies, and different visual touches. We'll teach you the pattern instead of the screenshot.

## The three ideas everything else builds on

1. **Borrowing is a shelf, not a store.** When you borrow, the title is checked out to *you* for a set number of days. Then it goes back, automatically. No fines. Fees don't exist here.
2. **Waitlists move.** If a book is checked out, you can place a hold. Fewer licenses = longer lines, but magazines, backlist titles, and most streaming-style items (like hoopla's instant content) usually have no line at all.
3. **Comfort is a setting, not a compromise.** Font size, dyslexia-friendly type, dark mode, line spacing, audio speed — all adjustable, most in two taps. Chapter 09 is the one worth reading with a cup of tea.

## What you'll need to get started

- A library card number and its PIN (chapter 01 tells you how to get both).
- A device with a Wi-Fi or data connection — phone, tablet, computer, e-reader, or TV.
- The name of your library, or the name of the town whose library you use.
- Roughly ten minutes for setup, and no patience beyond that. (We're serious. Setup takes less time than making dinner.)

## About the app names

- **Libby** is the reading app from **OverDrive**, the company most libraries use for ebooks, audiobooks, and digital magazines. If your library points you at Libby, that's this.
- **hoopla** is a second, separate platform. It's known for "no waiting" content, monthly borrowing allowances, and a broader menu (movies, TV, comics, music).
- **Send to Kindle** is a *feature* inside Libby (and the name of an unrelated Amazon tool for personal files). Chapter 04 untangles the two so you never wonder which one you're using.

Some libraries use one platform; some use both; a rare few use neither and instead give you access to a state-wide system or a different app entirely. Your card number works across all of them you're eligible for, once you add each system separately.
