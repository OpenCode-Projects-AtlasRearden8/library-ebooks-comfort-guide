# Send to Kindle, Without the Confusion

Kindle owners ask about one thing more than everything else combined: "how do I get *library* books onto my Kindle?" The answer is genuinely simple — but two different things are called "Send to Kindle," libraries vary, and Amazon inserts a sign-in at exactly the moment you're least patient. This chapter untangles all of it.

## The two different things named "Send to Kindle"

Know which is which and 90% of the confusion evaporates:

1. **Libby's "Read With Kindle"** — the library feature. After borrowing an ebook in Libby (from a U.S. library), you can have Libby hand the *library loan* to Amazon, which then appears on every Kindle device and Kindle app signed into your Amazon account. Loan rules stay the same: when the loan ends, Amazon removes the book automatically.
2. **Amazon's "Send to Kindle"** — a personal-files tool. It sends documents *you own* (PDF, EPUB, DOCX and similar, up to roughly 200 MB) from phone, computer, or browser into your Kindle library. It has nothing to do with libraries, and it can't read DRM-protected library book files.

If someone told you to "send the library EPUB to your Kindle with Send-to-Kindle" — that's the confusion in one sentence. It doesn't work, it was never supposed to work, and the good news is you don't need it. Use path 1 below.

## What you need

- A library card from a **U.S. library** that offers Kindle delivery (most do; a few consortia don't — if Libby's book menu never shows a Kindle option, your system skipped it, and you'll read in Libby instead; that's chapter 09's reader, and it's good).
- An **Amazon account** — free, and one you probably already have if you own a Kindle.
- Your Kindle device **connected to Wi-Fi** (or, if reading on your phone/tablet, the Kindle app installed and signed in).

U.S.-only is a licensing detail, not an app snobbery: Amazon and publishers structure Kindle loans for the U.S. market. Outside the U.S., Libby's own reader (and Kobo, chapter 12's checklist) is the practical path.

## The flow, exactly as it happens

The canonical path (verified against Libby's help pages, February 2026 wording):

1. **Borrow** the ebook in Libby (chapter 02). Or, if you already borrowed: tap **Shelf** → **Loans**.
2. Tap the book → tap **Read With...**
3. Tap **Kindle**.
4. Libby hands you to **Amazon's website** in a browser view. If prompted, **sign in to your Amazon account** — use the *same* account your Kindle device is registered to. (Different accounts is the #1 cause of "my book never showed up." Kindle books go to accounts, not devices directly.)
5. On Amazon's page, choose the **Deliver to:** device. Tap **Get Library Book**.
6. Done, mostly. The book appears on every Kindle device and Kindle app on that Amazon account — a minute or two of Wi-Fi sync later.

> **Tip — set the default once:** Libby → **Settings** → **Reading preference** (wording varies) → set new ebook borrowings to **Kindle** by default. From then on, borrowing fires the Kindle flow automatically and skips the "Read With..." shuffle.

## On the Kindle

The loan behaves like any Kindle book:

- Covers show on your Kindle's home screen once it syncs (**Connect/sync** happens automatically on Wi-Fi).
- Reading position syncs across Kindle devices/apps on the same account, including the Kindle app on your phone. Read the phone in line at the pharmacy, resume on the Paperwhite on the couch.
- All of Kindle's comfort settings work — font size, the OpenDyslexic font, dark mode on newer Kindles — see chapter 09.

When the library loan ends, Amazon *removes* the book from your devices automatically. If it lingers as a "ghost cover" (it occasionally does), syncing usually clears it. There are no late fees, because the loan just... ends.

## Early returns of Kindle loans

If you want the copy back in circulation before the due date, there are two clean ways:

1. **Return it in Libby** — Shelf → Loans → the book → **Return Early**. Libby tells Amazon, and the book disappears from your Kindles at their next sync.
2. **Return it on Amazon** — in your Amazon account's **Content & Devices** area, the loan shows as a library book with a **Return** option.

Both are fine. Doing either *frees the copy for the next person in line*, which is the polite endpoint of digital borrowing.

## "It won't send" — the four honest reasons

1. **Your library isn't a U.S. library** (or its Kindle option is off). Read in Libby instead.
2. **The publisher restricted Kindle lending for that title.** Libby will only offer in-app reading, or the Kindle option will be greyed out. Nothing is broken; choose another copy/format or the Libby reader.
3. **Amazon account mismatch** — you were signed into a different Amazon account than the one the Kindle is registered under. Sign out, sign into the right one, deliver again.
4. **The loan never actually went out / the device lost Wi-Fi.** See re-delivery below.

## The re-delivery fix (a proper "without confusion" trick)

The genuinely useful, least-known repair: you can re-deliver an active library loan from Amazon's own website. If a Kindle loan vanishes, fails to appear, or shows pages you can't open — don't re-borrow; deliver again:

1. On Amazon's website, go to **Content & Devices** (Account & Lists → Content & Devices).
2. Select **Books**.
3. Find the library book → **Deliver or Remove from Device**.
4. **To deliver:** pick your device(s) → **Make Changes**. If you'd previously delivered it to the device, deselect it → **Make Changes** → then reselect and deliver again (removing first, then re-adding, clears the stale copy).

The loan clock doesn't restart — re-delivering the *same active loan* just re-points it at your device. This fixes a surprising share of "the book broke" reports without any support calls.

## If Kindle is not your default, don't pretend

Some people genuinely read best in the Libby app: it has richer text customisation for dyslexia-focused reading (OpenDyslexic is available in both Libby and Kindle — chapter 09 compares), instant magazines, and the audiobook player all in one place. The right answer is whatever keeps *you* reading; send-to-Kindle is a convenience, not a rule.
