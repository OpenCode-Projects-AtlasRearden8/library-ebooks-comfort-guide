# Reading Comfortably: Big Text, Dyslexia-Friendly Fonts, and Dark Screens

Reading should be comfortable by default. It isn't, on factory settings — for anyone: eyes over 40, readers with dyslexia, night readers, anyone reading on a small phone in bright sun. The settings exist everywhere; they're buried two taps deep, and almost nobody sets them. This chapter sets them all. Nothing here is medical advice — these are reading-comfort controls, not treatments, and the proof of any font setting is a few pages with your own eyes.

## The settings that matter most, in order

If you change only one thing, make it **text size**. Then these, roughly in order of impact:

1. **Text size** — bigger than you think you need. Comfortable reading is neckless squinting at a bus timetable; larger text reduces eye strain for nearly everyone, not just "accessibility" users.
2. **Line spacing** — a small bump (1.2–1.5) makes dense text dramatically easier to track.
3. **Background/page colour** — soft sepia or dark mode for night reading; pure white in bright sun.
4. **Font** — dyslexia-friendly options (OpenDyslexic), heavier weights, and serif/sans choices all exist; try each for a chapter, not a paragraph.
5. **Brightness matching** — the screen should roughly match the room; auto-brightness does this for you on phones.

## Libby's reader (the most customisable)

In any book: **tap the centre of the screen → tap the reading-settings icon**. What you can set:

- **Text scale** — a slider; also a per-book size that carries to your other books. Turn on **Include accessibility sizes** to unlock *very* large sizes (this option is easy to miss and is the difference for lots of low-vision readers).
- **Book Design presets** — Libby ships a few attractive presets (Publisher's default, Legible, Paperback, Scholar, **OpenDyslexic**). The last one swaps in a font engineered to give dyslexic readers more distinct letterforms; it's been an option in Libby for years and remains the quickest start.
- **Custom design** — under a Custom option: choose the typeface, **line spacing** (up to 2.0), **justification** (left-aligned beats justified for many readers with dyslexia — rivers of white space disappear), and **text weight** (a heavier ink weight helps some readers considerably).
- **Page colours** — light, sepia, and dark backgrounds; brightness gets a slider; and the reader follows your device's dark mode when you tell it to.
- **Fixed-layout zoom** — for picture books, comics, and magazines you can zoom up to about 3.5×, which also does real work as a large-print mode for image-heavy titles.

Settings are per-reader and stick: open another book later and your last-used design reconnects automatically.

### Libby's app-wide accessibility settings

Under **☰ Menu → Settings → Accessibility** (wording shifted recently — on current versions it's on the Settings page), you'll find switches that change the *whole app*, not just the reader:

- **Reduce color variation** — neutral colours, stronger contrast, everywhere.
- **Reduce text variation** — one bold-enough weight app-wide (readers who find mixed weights rattling tend to love this one).
- **Label the navigation icons in the footer** — text labels under the icons ("Shelf," "Search") instead of symbols alone.
- **Screen-reader support** — Libby works with VoiceOver and TalkBack, and its own help section on reading with a screen reader is genuinely good.

Libby also honours your phone's own accessibility settings — larger system text, colour filters, reduced motion — which matters on the next section.

## The Kindle app and Kindle e-readers

The Kindle app and Kindle devices carry their own font stack, independent of Libby:

- **Font size** — the "Aa" control in any book; Kindle offers a clean ladder of sizes plus several margin/wide-layout options.
- **Fonts** — the list includes **OpenDyslexic** alongside the classics (Bookerly, Georgia…). Same advice: try each on a real page for a chapter.
- **Themes** — white, cream/sepia, dark; brightness per device; on Paperwhites the warm/amber tone nightly reduces blue-light squint.
- **Layout extras** — bold text options on newer devices/software, adjustable margins/line spacing, and left-justified text on recent versions.

The same OpenDyslexic font recommendation applies on the Kindle e-reader (search your device's fonts; newer firmware ships it).

## hoopla's reader

hoopla's ebook reader is more basic — fonts/text formatting options are lighter than Libby and Kindle. Practical advice:

- On **phones/tablets**, hoopla's text size control does exist in the reader (a size toggle with limited steps).
- If you need **truly large text**, prefer Libby (or the Kindle flow) for books, and use hoopla mainly for audiobooks/comics — where its player and panel reader are strong and text size isn't the issue (audio has *speed* & volume; comics are zoomed panels).
- hoopla's audiobook player has the sleep timer and speed control discussed in chapter 07; those are its "comfort" controls.

## The e-ink readers (Kindle, Kobo)

E-readers remain the comfort champions for long sessions — no backlight glow fatigue, weeks of battery, and sunlight-proof.

- **Kindle e-readers:** the same font menu as the app (including OpenDyslexic); text size goes larger than most people ever try — it's worth trying; bold fonts exist on recent models.
- **Kobo e-readers:** most current Kobo models (Clara, Libra, Elipsa families; a couple of others) have OverDrive/Libby support built in for U.S./Canada libraries — browse, borrow, and read library books right on the device (chapter 10's checklist covers setup). Kobo's reading settings include font size, font family, line spacing, margins, weight, and dark-mode-ish inversion on some models; look for a dyslexia-friendly font among the choices on your model. Two limits stated plainly: **Kobo devices can't play Libby audiobooks or show magazines**, and a stubborn minority of titles won't load on the device at all (read those in the Libby app).

## The phone's own settings (helps every app at once)

Before hunting per-app settings, set the phone once:

- **System text size + bold text** (iPhone: Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size; Android: Settings → Display → Font size). apps like Libby honour it.
- **Screen zoom / display size** for permanently larger UI.
- **Dark mode** + scheduled bedtime dimming; **auto-brightness** on.
- Increase **contrast** if colours look washed out; colour filters exist if you've had a suggestion from *your* eye-care provider.

> **A kind word about dyslexia and fonts:** OpenDyslexic and friends help many readers; spacing and weight changes help others; some prefer a plain large serif font. Evidence is mixed font-by-font, so the honest rule is: whichever setting makes *you* turn pages easier is the correct one, full stop. Try each for a while. (And no app can claim to treat dyslexia — these are comfort tools, not therapy.)

## A two-minute starter kit (if you change nothing else)

1. Phone: system text one size larger than "medium," auto-brightness on.
2. Libby reader: text scale up two notches; enable *Include accessibility sizes*; page = sepia at night.
3. Audiobooks: sleep timer 15 minutes, bookmark habit, downloads before the commute.
4. Kindle: OpenDyslexic trial for one chapter; keep whichever won.

You'll do this once per device and never think about it again — which is the point. Next: a device-by-device checklist that turns all of the above into five-minute setups.
